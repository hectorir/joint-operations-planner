# Friendly COG — Exercise: Pacific Cyclone

> Fictional UNCLASSIFIED training scenario.

## Strategic / operational context
The friendly side of this operation is a coalition centered on the **Government of Cernavia** as the recovery's legitimate authority, with **USAID/DART** as the USG lead and **DoD** in support. Allied partners (Aurelia, Verdania) and IGOs/NGOs (IFRC, MSF, WFP) operate within or alongside this coalition. The COG must serve the operation's central objective: HN-led recovery within 90 days.

## End state / objectives this COG serves
- End state: Cernavia government and USAID can sustain the recovery without DoD presence within 90 days.
- Key objective: HN visibly leads recovery.

## Center of Gravity
**COG: The Government of Cernavia's perceived legitimacy as the lead authority for the recovery.**

## Why this is the COG (not a critical capability)
Without HN legitimacy, the recovery cannot be sustained — even an efficient external response collapses into a permanent dependency or a vacuum the criminal network fills. Other strengths (US lift, allied ships, NGO presence) feed into this COG; they do not substitute for it.

## Critical Capabilities

| ID | Critical Capability | Why essential |
|---|---|---|
| CC-1 | HN visible delivery of essential services | Population must see HN providing for them |
| CC-2 | Coherent HN public communication | Fills information vacuum; counters criminal-network narratives |
| CC-3 | HN security forces coordinating with US/allies | Force protection achievable without US-dominant posture |

## Critical Requirements

| ID | Critical Requirement | Supports CC | Source / Provider |
|---|---|---|---|
| CR-1 | Functional HN distribution chain | CC-1 | HN logistics + USAID enabling |
| CR-2 | HN spokespeople with reach | CC-2 | HN govt + IO cell amplification |
| CR-3 | Functional HN police presence in affected districts | CC-3 | HN police + UN/HN security cooperation |

## Critical Vulnerabilities (of the friendly COG)

| ID | Critical Vulnerability | Targets CR | Risk to mitigate |
|---|---|---|---|
| CV-F1 | Distribution chain depends on CER-A and CER-P | CR-1 | Single-point infrastructure risk |
| CV-F2 | HN spokespeople may be slow to mobilize | CR-2 | Information vacuum exploited by adversaries |
| CV-F3 | HN police capacity stressed across all districts | CR-3 | Security gaps create criminal-network entry |

## Friendly-COG protection actions
- Operate in HN-visible/US-invisible posture (mitigates CV-F1's perception risk and CV-F2's narrative risk).
- IO cell partnered with HN voices, not US voices (CV-F2).
- Force protection scaled to threat, not to "defense in depth" (CV-F3).

## Completion check
1. Will operations succeed if the friendly COG is preserved? **Yes** — HN legitimacy preserved enables 90-day sustainable recovery.
2. Does mission accomplishment serve the next-higher objective (US Indo-Pacific commitment signaling)? **Yes** — partner-first response strengthens the alliance value proposition.
3. If the COG is degraded (e.g., HN seen as failing), what emerges? Permanent dependency or criminal-network legitimacy capture. Both are unacceptable.
4. Should we strengthen this COG? **Yes — explicitly.** Every plan element should ask: does this strengthen or weaken HN legitimacy?
