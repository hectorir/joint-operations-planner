# Threat COG — Black Reef Syndicate (fictional)

> Fictional UNCLASSIFIED training scenario.

## Strategic / operational context
The "threat" in this scenario is the **Black Reef Syndicate**, a transnational criminal network exploiting HADR conditions for trafficking, looting, and influence-buying. There is no state adversary in the scenario. Per JP 5-0 §8.2, COG analysis still applies because there is an adversarial relationship — the syndicate competes for the same legitimacy space the friendly COG occupies.

## Objectives this COG serves (the syndicate's, not ours)
- Establish parallel distribution networks for fuel, medicine, and information.
- Buy local political influence during recovery.
- Lock in long-term presence under cover of legitimate-looking operations.

## Center of Gravity
**COG: The syndicate's local-broker network — the trusted human intermediaries who connect the syndicate to populations, businesses, and minor officials.**

## Why this is the COG (not the syndicate's leadership)
Leadership is replaceable; the broker network is not. Without trusted local brokers, the syndicate cannot move product, control narratives, or buy influence. The friendly task is not to "destroy the syndicate" (outside our authorities) but to **deny brokers the conditions they exploit** — fast HN distribution, HN-visible spokespeople, present HN police.

## Critical Capabilities

| ID | Critical Capability | Why essential |
|---|---|---|
| CC-T1 | Move scarce goods (fuel, medicine) faster than HN/USAID | The wedge that opens broker access |
| CC-T2 | Control rumor flow in affected districts | Substitute narrative for HN/USAID information |
| CC-T3 | Buy minor officials and small business owners | Establishes the broker network's local reach |

## Critical Requirements

| ID | Critical Requirement | Supports CC |
|---|---|---|
| CR-T1 | Information vacuum in affected districts | CC-T1, CC-T2 |
| CR-T2 | Distribution gaps lasting >48 hours | CC-T1 |
| CR-T3 | Cash flow + reputation among small officials | CC-T3 |

## Critical Vulnerabilities

| ID | Critical Vulnerability | Targets CR | Criticality | Accessibility | Redundancy | Escalation risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CV-T1 | Their broker network requires *time and gaps* to set in | CR-T1, CR-T2 | High | High (we can compress time) | Low (no parallel network ready) | Low |
| CV-T2 | Their narrative requires *unfilled information space* | CR-T1 | High | High (IO cell) | Medium (some narrative pre-positioned) | Low |
| CV-T3 | Their cash-for-influence loop is visible to HN police | CR-T3 | Medium | Medium (HN action, not US) | Medium | Low |

## Approach selection
**Indirect approach (§7.7).** We do not target the syndicate directly — outside our authorities and high escalation cost in a HADR scenario. Instead, we deny their critical requirements:
- **Compress distribution time** (CV-T1, CV-T2): fast restoration of CER-A; HNS via fishing fleet for last-mile.
- **Fill the information space** (CV-T2): IO cell amplifying HN spokespeople from Day 1.
- **Strengthen HN police presence** (CV-T3): CDF + US/allied advisors visible alongside HN police, not in lieu of them.

## Completion check
1. Will the joint force achieve its objectives if these CVs are exploited (broker network never sets in)? **Yes** — HN legitimacy preserved; recovery sustainable.
2. Does mission accomplishment serve the next-higher objective? **Yes** — see friendly COG.
3. If brokers do set in despite our actions, what emerges? Long-tail criminal influence in the recovery economy; degrades HN legitimacy. Trigger condition: PIR-04 indicates broker activity exceeds threshold.
4. Direct attack feasible/desirable? **No** — outside US authorities; high escalation; HADR character violated.
5. Strengthen our own COG (HN legitimacy)? **Yes** — see `cog/friendly.md`.
