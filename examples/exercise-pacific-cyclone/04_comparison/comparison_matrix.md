## Three-question summary

### 1. What are the differences between each COA?
- **COA-01** balances speed and visibility with a small ground/maritime force.
- **COA-02** over-delivers force, trading HN visibility and Pacific posture for capacity.
- **COA-03** minimizes US footprint by going maritime-only — best for visibility, worst for interior coverage.

### 2. Advantages and disadvantages
- **COA-01:** Best balance; preserves friendly COG; modest sustainment risk. Disadvantage: still has ground footprint (a small target surface).
- **COA-02:** Most capacity; fastest possible at large scale. Disadvantages: degrades friendly COG, draws on Pacific posture.
- **COA-03:** Lowest footprint; protects friendly COG. Disadvantage: under-delivers in interior districts where HN ground capacity is degraded.

### 3. Risks
- **COA-01:** Sustainment chain stress in weeks 4–8 if allied ships are delayed.
- **COA-02:** Strategic risk to deterrence framing.
- **COA-03:** Population-coverage gap in interior districts.

## Tie-breaking note

COA-01 and COA-03 tied at 4.55. The wargame against the most-dangerous case (compound crisis) was the decider: COA-01 retained ground access for engineer remediation post-earthquake; COA-03 would have required a major plan change. Recommend COA-01.

## Staff recommendation

The staff recommends **COA-01 (small-jtf-fast)**. The commander is not bound by this recommendation per JP 5-0 §5.7.
