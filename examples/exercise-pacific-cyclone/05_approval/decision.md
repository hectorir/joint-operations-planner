## Acceptable risk

**Risk-to-mission (operational risk):** Moderate. The principal mission risk is degradation of host-nation legitimacy if US visibility creeps; the plan's HN-visible / US-invisible posture is the primary mitigation. A secondary risk is sustainment chain stress in weeks 4–8 if allied ship arrivals slip; this is mitigated by HNS via Cernavian fishing fleet for last-mile distribution and by maintaining 7-day HN delivery buffer.

**Risk-to-force (force management risk):** Low. The JTF is sourced primarily from allocated forces with minimal draw on Pacific steady-state posture. Service component rotation cycles are unaffected at the 90-day plan horizon.

**Residual risk after mitigation:** I accept the moderate residual risk to mission and the low residual risk to force. The principal residual risk is a compound natural disaster (B-EQ) that the plan now addresses via branch.

## Rationale

COA-01 best protects the friendly center of gravity — host-nation legitimacy — while delivering effective HADR support to USAID/DART. It does so without degrading Pacific deterrence posture (a SecDef-stated constraint). The wargame against the compound-crisis case validated COA-01's robustness when augmented by branch B-EQ; COA-03 (maritime-only) would have failed under that scenario by losing access to interior districts. COA-02 (large/deliberate) was rejected because its size actively damages the friendly COG.

I am explicitly prioritizing host-nation visibility over delivery efficiency where the two trade against each other. Subordinate commanders must read this priority into their decisions.
