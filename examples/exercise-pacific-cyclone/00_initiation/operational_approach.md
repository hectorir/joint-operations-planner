# Operational Approach — Initial Sketch

> Fictional UNCLASSIFIED training scenario. This is the Step 1 sketch; refined version lives at `01_mission_analysis/operational_approach.md`.

## 1. Strategic direction and guidance
See `strategic_guidance.md`.

## 2. Strategic environment
- Indo-Pacific region; alliance management is a constant.
- Strategic competitors monitor US response speed and conduct as a signal of commitment.
- US public attention on the disaster will be brief; political risk grows after week 4 if presence is still visible.

## 3. Operational environment
- **Physical:** Cernavia is an island state, area ~2,400 km², capital Port Cernavia. Single major airfield, single major port. Mountainous interior limits ground transit.
- **Informational:** Cernavian government broadcasting is degraded; SMS networks intermittent; rumors will fill information vacuums.
- **PMESII summary:**
  - Political: government legitimate but stretched; presidential statement of partnership with US.
  - Military: Cernavian Defense Force (CDF) ~2,000 personnel, conducting search-and-rescue and security with US/allied support.
  - Economic: tourism and fisheries; both will be flat for 6+ months.
  - Social: tight-knit population; high in-group trust, low trust of outsiders unless visibly partnered with their own.
  - Information: strong rumor culture; vacuum will be filled by criminal-network narratives if HN/USAID don't fill it first.
  - Infrastructure: severely degraded along the coast; functional in interior.
- **Other actors:** Black Reef Syndicate (fictional criminal network); IFRC, MSF, WFP; allied navies (Aurelia, Verdania).

## 4. Problem definition
**Hypothesis:** The mission risk is *not* primarily logistical. It is a legitimacy problem: every action that signals "the Americans are running this" undermines the Cernavian government's standing, creates space for the criminal network's narrative, and reduces the durability of recovery. The plan must therefore optimize for HN-visible / US-invisible delivery, even when this trades short-term efficiency.

## 5. Initial assumptions (deferred to mission analysis)
- USAID/DART takes the USG lead and remains lead throughout.
- HN government remains coherent and cooperative.
- Criminal network does not escalate to direct attacks on US personnel.

## 6. Operational approach (the broad action)
A small, fast Joint Task Force (JTF-CERNAVIA) deploys within 72 hours of approval, providing lift, engineer, medical, and C2 support to USAID/DART operating with the Cernavian government. The JTF maintains a low public profile. Maritime support (a single L-class amphib + a hospital ship) provides reach without a large in-country footprint. The JTF's presence draws down by week 8 with the goal of zero DoD footprint by day 90.

## 7. External decision points
- DP-A: USAID/DART transition from "deliver" to "enable" — triggers shift of main effort.
- DP-B: Criminal network displacement attempts material — triggers HN/US coordination on information operations.
- DP-C: HN government communicates self-sustainment readiness — triggers DoD drawdown sequence.

## 9. Planning and assessment guidance
- Assessment battle rhythm: weekly Assessment Working Group with USAID/DART representation.
- Decision cycle: every 72 hours during weeks 1–4, then weekly.
- Critical MOE: HN-attributed delivery share (we want HN attribution to climb; USAID/DoD attribution to fall).
